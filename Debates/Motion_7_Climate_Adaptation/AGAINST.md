# AGAINST the Motion: "Adaptation of humans to climate change is occurring fast enough to keep pace with the accelerating, unprecedented scale of climate-related crises"

## Speech (~8 minutes)

---

Ladies and gentlemen,

In the Pacific Ocean, there is a country called **Tuvalu.** It has 11,000 citizens, a culture stretching back thousands of years, and a land area smaller than Manhattan. By the end of this century, Tuvalu **will no longer exist.** The ocean will swallow it — every home, every school, every grave. And in 2023, Australia's response to this existential catastrophe was to offer Tuvalu's citizens a **visa** — the world's first climate visa. A permission slip to abandon their homeland.

That is not adaptation keeping pace with climate change. That is **surrender dressed up as innovation.**

And Tuvalu is not an outlier. It is a preview. A preview of what happens when the greatest crisis in human history meets a response that is too slow, too small, and too late.

I stand firmly against this motion. **Human adaptation to climate change is not occurring fast enough.** The gap between the scale of the crisis and the scale of the response is not closing — it is **widening.** And every year we tell ourselves we are keeping pace, we fall further behind.

---

### 1. The Funding Gap Is Not a Gap — It Is a Chasm

My first argument is about numbers — and the numbers are devastating.

The **United Nations Development Programme** reports that developing countries' adaptation needs are **10 to 18 times larger** than currently available public finance. Let that ratio sink in. Not twice as large. Not three times. **Ten to eighteen times.** If your house were on fire and you had one-tenth of the water you needed, you would not describe your firefighting effort as "keeping pace."

The **IMF's Finance & Development** journal, authored by **Adam Behsudi**, quantifies the gap precisely: annual adaptation costs in developing economies are expected to rise to **$140 to $300 billion by 2030.** The actual amount of adaptation financing flowing to these countries? A mere **$30 billion on average annually.** That is a shortfall of over **$100 billion every single year** — and the gap is growing, not shrinking.

And here is what makes this even worse: **91% of all climate funding** in 2021-2022 was directed toward **mitigation** — reducing emissions — not toward adaptation. Only **9 cents of every climate dollar** goes to helping people survive the impacts that are already happening. As the **Gold Standard** reports, a mere **1.6% of adaptation funds** come from the private sector. The world is not just underfunding adaptation — it is **structurally starving it** of resources.

The panelist from the model debate who argues for the motion will point to impressive-sounding investment returns and market potential. But potential is not reality. The reality is a 10-to-18-fold gap between what is needed and what exists. That is not keeping pace. That is falling catastrophically behind.

---

### 2. The Scale of the Crisis Is Unprecedented — and Accelerating

My second argument asks the FOR side to reckon with the sheer, unprecedented scale of what adaptation is supposed to keep pace with.

According to **Earth.org**, global CO2 emissions have increased **182-fold** — from 204 million tonnes in 1850 to **37,100 million tonnes in 2022.** The planet is now **1.3 degrees Celsius warmer** than pre-industrial levels, and the rate of warming is accelerating. The **Qatar Debate Journal** notes that while global temperatures rose 1.18 degrees over a century, the majority of that warming occurred in just the last **40 years.**

What does this mean in human terms? Natural disasters exacerbated by warming now kill approximately **60,000 people every year.** The Qatar Debate Journal estimates **150,000 annual deaths** from climate-change-related causes more broadly. These are not projections — these are people dying **right now**, today, while the FOR side tells us adaptation is keeping pace.

And the trajectory is terrifying. The UNDP warns: **"With every fraction of a degree of warming, the impacts of climate change will become more frequent and more intense — and adaptation will become that much harder and more expensive."** This is the cruel paradox of adaptation: the longer we take to address the root cause, the more it costs to manage the symptoms — and the less effective those symptoms management becomes.

The FOR side will celebrate adaptation successes. But celebrating Tuvalu's 780 meters of reclaimed sand while the entire country faces submersion by century's end is like celebrating a new paint job on the Titanic.

---

### 3. There Are Hard Limits to What Adaptation Can Achieve

My third argument confronts the most uncomfortable truth in this debate: **there are aspects of climate change that no amount of adaptation can protect us from.**

Climate scientist **Adam H. Sobel**, writing in **TIME Magazine**, states this with scientific precision: there are **"too many aspects of climate change that no plausible adaptation can protect us from."** When a coral reef ecosystem dies from ocean acidification, no adaptation rebuilds it. When a glacier that feeds a river system for 100 million people disappears, no engineering project replaces it. When permafrost thaws and releases millennia of stored methane, no early warning system stops the feedback loop.

Consider the small island developing states. **Earth.org** reports that **Tuvalu risks disappearing completely by the end of the century.** These nations face what the report calls a **"limited ability to relocate people"** — because where do you relocate an entire country? An entire culture? Australia's climate visa is not adaptation. It is an admission that adaptation has **failed.**

The **Qatar Debate Journal** acknowledges this directly: **"Some problems we cannot adapt to, especially health damage caused by climate change factors."** Sea-level rise walls offer only temporary solutions — the ocean keeps rising. Heatwaves that exceed human physiological tolerance cannot be "adapted to" by communities without air conditioning. Crop failures in regions that become too hot or too dry to farm cannot be solved by climate-smart agriculture alone — some land will simply become uninhabitable.

The FOR side treats adaptation as if it were infinitely elastic — as if human ingenuity has no ceiling. But physics does not negotiate. Biology does not compromise. There are **hard planetary boundaries** beyond which adaptation is meaningless, and we are approaching them faster than at any point in human history.

---

### 4. The Most Vulnerable Are Being Left Behind

My fourth argument exposes the profound inequity at the heart of the adaptation story — an inequity that the FOR side's optimistic statistics systematically obscure.

**Earth.org** reports that **"least developed countries and small island developing states are shown to be less resilient to climate shocks"** — the very nations that contributed least to the crisis are the ones least equipped to survive it. These countries possess **"limited financial resources, making it difficult to adapt effectively."** They lack specialized expertise in climate-risk planning. Their monitoring and evaluation systems are fragmented. Coordination between government sectors is poor.

And even wealthy nations are not immune. Earth.org notes that industrialized nations' adaptation capability **"does not always translate into effective action."** The United States — the richest country on Earth — was, according to the **White House's own assessment**, **"behind" on adaptation science development.** If America is behind, what hope do Bangladesh, Chad, or Haiti have?

The **IMF** data makes the inequity quantitative: adaptation costs in developing economies are **$70 billion annually** and rising to **$140 to $300 billion by 2030.** These countries cannot fund this alone. And the **Gold Standard** confirms that the private sector — which commands the capital needed — contributes only **1.6%** of adaptation finance. The market has spoken, and its answer to the world's most vulnerable people is: **you are not a profitable investment.**

The UNDP, even while showcasing its own projects, concedes that despite reaching 164 million people, developing nations demonstrate "outsized ambition" only **"despite limited resources"** — a diplomatic way of saying they are making heroic efforts with almost nothing. Heroism is admirable. But it is not a substitute for adequate resources — and adequate resources are nowhere in sight.

---

### 5. Treating Symptoms While the Disease Accelerates

My fifth argument addresses the fundamental logical flaw in the FOR side's position: **you cannot adapt your way out of a crisis whose root cause you are not addressing.**

The **IMF** reports that **four-fifths of global emissions are not priced.** The global average emissions price is a pathetic **$3 per ton** — against the **$75 per ton** that the IMF itself says is needed to limit warming to 2 degrees Celsius. That is a **25-fold shortfall** in addressing the root cause of the crisis that adaptation is supposed to keep pace with.

Fossil fuels still account for **over 50% of global electricity** in 2024, according to the **World Resources Institute.** CO2 emissions have increased 182-fold since 1850 and show no sign of meaningful decline. The Qatar Debate Journal makes the point explicitly: adaptation expenses **"double to quadruple within 10 years"** if mitigation is delayed. Every year the world fails to cut emissions, the cost of adaptation grows — and the odds that adaptation can keep pace shrink.

**Adam H. Sobel** in TIME warns that some adaptation framing can actually serve as **"cover for minimizing accountability"** for carbon pollution. When we celebrate adaptation victories while emissions continue to rise, we create a dangerous illusion of progress. We tell ourselves the house is fine because we have bought better smoke detectors — while pouring gasoline on the fire in the basement.

Professor **Auroop Ganguly** of Northeastern University — often cited by adaptation optimists — himself calls the debate between adaptation and mitigation a **"false argument."** Both, he insists, are **"necessary for safety and security."** His point is precisely that adaptation alone is not enough — and therefore adaptation alone is not keeping pace. It cannot. It was never designed to.

---

### Conclusion

Let me return to where I began — to Tuvalu, that tiny nation building new land from sand as the ocean rises around it.

The FOR side will tell you that Tuvalu's 780 meters of reclaimed coastline represent human ingenuity triumphing over adversity. I tell you that they represent **11,000 people fighting for survival with sand and concrete while the rest of the world pumps 37 billion tonnes of CO2 into the atmosphere every year.**

The adaptation funding gap is **10 to 18 times** what is available. **91%** of climate finance goes elsewhere. **60,000 people** die every year from climate-amplified disasters. Entire nations face extinction. And the root cause — fossil fuel emissions — remains barely addressed, with four-fifths of global emissions completely unpriced.

Adaptation is not keeping pace. It is not close to keeping pace. And every year we pretend otherwise, we lose time we do not have.

The climate scientist Adam Sobel is right: there are **"too many aspects of climate change that no plausible adaptation can protect us from."** The question is not whether we should adapt — of course we should. The question is whether adaptation is **enough** — whether it is occurring **fast enough** to match the accelerating, unprecedented scale of the crisis. And the answer — measured in dollars, in degrees, in deaths, and in disappearing nations — is an unequivocal **no.**

Vote against this motion. Thank you.
