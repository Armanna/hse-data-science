# FOR the Motion: "It is environmental factors (parenting styles, educational experiences, cultural backgrounds, etc.) that play a greater role in an individual's development than biological heredity"

## Speech (~8 minutes)

---

Ladies and gentlemen,

In 1960, **South Korea** was one of the poorest countries on Earth — its GDP per capita was lower than that of most sub-Saharan African nations, and its literacy rates were dismal. The genetic makeup of the Korean population was no different from what it had been for centuries. And yet, within a single generation — through massive investment in education, a cultural transformation of attitudes toward learning, and systematic environmental enrichment — South Korea became one of the **highest-performing countries in the world** on every measure of cognitive and academic achievement.

The Korean gene pool did not change. **The environment did.** And with it, the trajectory of an entire nation — tens of millions of people — was rewritten. That is not a minor environmental effect. That is environment as the dominant force in human development, demonstrated at the scale of an entire civilization.

I stand firmly in support of this motion: **environmental factors — parenting styles, educational experiences, cultural backgrounds — play a greater role in an individual's development than biological heredity.** Not because genes do not matter — they do — but because the evidence overwhelmingly shows that it is environment that activates, shapes, suppresses, and ultimately determines what our genetic potential becomes.

---

### 1. The Science of Epigenetics Has Rewritten the Nature-Nurture Debate

My first argument draws on the revolutionary field of **epigenetics** — the science that has fundamentally changed our understanding of how genes and environment interact.

The traditional view was simple: you inherit genes, and those genes determine your traits. But **epigenetics** has revealed that genes are not fixed instructions — they are more like a **script that the environment directs.** Environmental factors — nutrition, stress, toxins, parenting behavior, social experience — physically alter how genes are expressed by attaching or removing chemical markers (methyl groups) to DNA, turning genes "on" or "off" without changing the underlying genetic code.

The landmark research by **Michael Meaney** at McGill University demonstrated this with stunning clarity. Rat pups raised by attentive, nurturing mothers developed fundamentally different stress responses than pups raised by neglectful mothers — **even when the pups were genetically identical.** The nurturing behavior physically altered the epigenetic markers on the glucocorticoid receptor gene, permanently changing how the offspring's brains processed stress. This was not nature versus nurture — it was **nurture rewriting nature.**

And this applies to humans. Research published in **Nature Neuroscience** has shown that childhood abuse leaves **epigenetic scars** — measurable chemical changes to genes governing the stress response system — that persist into adulthood and can even be transmitted to the next generation. The **Dutch Hunger Winter** studies demonstrated that famine experienced by pregnant women in 1944-45 caused epigenetic changes in their children that were still detectable **six decades later** — and in their grandchildren.

The panelist from the model debate who argues against the motion will likely present genetics as a fixed blueprint. But modern science tells us that the blueprint is **editable** — and the editor is the environment. Genes load the gun; environment pulls the trigger.

---

### 2. Twin Studies — The Gold Standard — Actually Support Environmental Influence

My second argument confronts what the opposition will consider their strongest evidence: **twin studies.**

Opponents of this motion love twin studies. Identical twins share 100% of their DNA, so any differences between them must be environmental. And here is what those studies actually show: for most complex human traits — personality, intelligence, mental health, behavior — **genetics accounts for approximately 40 to 60 percent of the variation.** That means environment accounts for **40 to 60 percent as well.**

But here is the critical nuance the opposition misses. The landmark **Minnesota Study of Twins Reared Apart**, led by **Thomas Bouchard**, found that identical twins raised in different environments showed remarkable similarities in some traits — but also **striking differences.** Twins separated at birth and raised in different socioeconomic conditions showed IQ differences of **up to 20 points** — a gap large enough to separate "average" from "gifted" in educational classification. The same genes, radically different outcomes — because of environment.

Moreover, as behavioral geneticist **Eric Turkheimer** demonstrated in his famous 2003 study published in **Psychological Science**, the heritability of IQ **varies dramatically by socioeconomic status.** In wealthy families, where environmental conditions are uniformly good, genes explain most of the variation in IQ — because the environment is held constant. But in impoverished families, **environment overwhelms genetics entirely.** Turkheimer found that among the poorest families, shared environment accounted for **nearly 60% of IQ variation**, while genetic factors accounted for almost nothing.

This means that the opposition's heritability statistics are not measuring fixed biological destiny. They are measuring **how much environmental variation exists in a given population.** When environments are unequal — as they are in the real world — environment dominates.

---

### 3. Cross-Cultural Evidence Demolishes Genetic Determinism

My third argument moves from the laboratory to the real world — and the real world provides overwhelming evidence for the primacy of environment.

Consider the **Flynn Effect** — the well-documented phenomenon that average IQ scores have risen **approximately 3 points per decade** across every population studied since the early 20th century. In the Netherlands, the average IQ rose by **21 points between 1952 and 1982.** In Kenya, children in 1984 scored the equivalent of **26 points higher** than children in the same communities in 1998.

Human genetics did not change in 30 years. **Environments did.** Better nutrition, expanded education, greater cognitive stimulation, urbanization, reduced infectious disease — these environmental transformations produced measurable, dramatic increases in the trait that the opposition claims is primarily genetic.

Cross-cultural comparisons are equally powerful. **East Asian academic achievement** — often attributed by biological determinists to genetic superiority — has been convincingly explained by environmental factors. Research by **Harold Stevenson** and **James Stigler** at the University of Michigan showed that the achievement gap between American and Japanese students was almost entirely attributable to **differences in educational practices, parenting expectations, and cultural attitudes toward effort.** When Asian children are raised in Western environments without the cultural emphasis on academic diligence, their achievement levels converge with local norms.

Similarly, **gender differences** in mathematical ability — once attributed to innate biological sex differences — have been shown to vary dramatically across cultures. In countries with greater gender equality, such as **Iceland and Sweden**, the math gender gap **virtually disappears.** The same genes, expressed in different cultural environments, produce entirely different outcomes.

If biology were the primary driver, these cross-cultural differences would not exist. The fact that they do — consistently, across every trait studied — proves that environment is the dominant force shaping human development.

---

### 4. The Power of Education and Intervention Programs

My fourth argument presents the most practically important evidence in this debate: the documented power of **environmental interventions** to transform human development — even against supposedly fixed genetic baselines.

The **Perry Preschool Project**, launched in Ypsilanti, Michigan in 1962, enrolled disadvantaged African American children in an intensive preschool program. Researchers tracked these children for **over 50 years.** The results were extraordinary: participants were **more likely to graduate high school, more likely to be employed, earned higher incomes, and were less likely to be arrested** than a control group with identical demographic and genetic backgrounds. A two-year environmental intervention produced measurable benefits **half a century later.**

The **Abecedarian Project** in North Carolina achieved similar results. Children from impoverished families who received intensive early childhood education from birth through age five showed **IQ gains of 5 to 10 points** that persisted into adulthood — along with higher rates of college attendance, later ages of first parenthood, and better physical health. These were children with the same genetic backgrounds as the control group. The only difference was environment.

At a national scale, **South Korea's** transformation is extraordinary. In 1960, South Korea was one of the poorest countries in the world, with literacy rates comparable to sub-Saharan Africa. Within a single generation — through massive investment in education, cultural transformation of attitudes toward learning, and systematic environmental enrichment — South Korea became one of the world's highest-performing countries on every measure of cognitive and academic achievement. The gene pool did not change. The environment did.

The panelist arguing against the motion cannot explain these results through genetics. You cannot genetically transform a population in 30 years. What you can do — and what these examples prove — is transform outcomes by transforming environments.

---

### 5. The Dangerous Implications of Genetic Determinism

My fifth argument moves from evidence to ethics — because the question of whether environment or heredity matters more is not merely academic. It has **profound consequences for how we organize society.**

If we accept the position that biological heredity plays the greater role in development, we are logically committed to a set of deeply troubling conclusions. If intelligence is primarily genetic, then investing in education for disadvantaged children is largely futile. If criminal behavior is primarily heritable, then rehabilitation programs are wasted resources. If mental illness is primarily genetic, then therapy and social support are secondary to pharmacology.

History shows where genetic determinism leads. The **eugenics movement** of the early 20th century — which forcibly sterilized tens of thousands of people in the United States and culminated in the horrors of Nazi Germany — was built on precisely this premise: that heredity determines worth, and environment is powerless to change what nature has decreed.

We know this is wrong. **Adopted children** raised in enriching environments consistently outperform predictions based on their biological parents' characteristics. Children from orphanages who are adopted into loving families show dramatic **IQ gains of 12 to 18 points.** Former child soldiers reintegrated into supportive communities recover from trauma that genetic determinism would declare permanent. The human capacity for transformation through environmental change is not just scientifically documented — it is one of the most hopeful facts about our species.

The motion before us is not asking whether genes exist. Of course they do. It is asking which factor plays the **greater role.** And when the evidence shows that environments can produce 20-point IQ swings, that cultural change can eliminate gender gaps, that early intervention can reshape life trajectories for 50 years, and that epigenetics reveals environment literally rewriting genetic expression — the answer is clear.

---

### Conclusion

Let me close with a thought experiment.

Take two genetically identical twins at birth. Place one in a loving, educated, well-nourished family in Copenhagen. Place the other in an orphanage with minimal stimulation, poor nutrition, and no emotional attachment in a war-torn country. Return in 18 years.

You will not find two similar people. You will find two people so different — in intelligence, personality, health, emotional regulation, social skills, language ability, and life expectancy — that without genetic testing, you would never guess they were related, let alone identical.

The genes are the same. **Everything else is different.** And it is everything else — the environment — that made the difference.

Epigenetics shows us that environment rewrites genetic expression. Twin studies show that heritability collapses in unequal environments. The Flynn Effect proves that environmental change transforms cognitive ability across entire populations. Intervention programs demonstrate that changing environments changes lives — for decades. And cross-cultural evidence reveals that traits we once attributed to biology are shaped by culture, education, and opportunity.

Genes provide the raw material. But it is environment — parenting, education, culture, nutrition, opportunity — that builds the person. And the builder matters more than the bricks.

I urge you to support this motion. Thank you.
