HOW TO PREPARE IF YOU ARE A PANELIST


Study the preparation materials, take notes of the arguments from both sides.
Analyze them.
Provide a critical evaluation of the suggested argumentation: its strength and validity, logical fallacies, and the rhetoric style of the speaker.
Prepare your panel speech; it has to be 7 minutes long (+1-2 minutes, in exceptional cases).
Remember to state your position upfront, at the very beginning of the speech. Make it a memorable and ear-catching statement.
IMPORTANT: your speech has to be designed as a virtual debate with the panelists from the model debate and from the authors of the reading. That is, you have to appeal to their arguments, provide their analysis, argue / side with them, offer additional evidence, etc.
IN CLASS, you should be listening to the speeches of the other panelists, keeping track of their arguments. When your turn to speak arrives, appeal to the arguments of your co-panelists (from either side). That is, discuss their arguments, provide their analysis, argue / side with them, offer additional evidence, etc.
Remember the formal requirement: your speech is to contain at least FOUR EXPLICIT appeals to the argumentation from the preparation materials (the model video and the reading) and at least TWO EXPLICIT appeals to the argumentation from the previous speakers of your panel. EXPLICIT means, you should state the author of the argument, give a brief recap, and provide a critical analysis of the argument.
In conclusion of your speech, it is a good idea to restate your stance and call on the audience to vote for your position.
Prepare to take the questions from the audience in the second round (Q&A).
Note: you are welcome to accompany your speech with presentation slides; yet, this is not an obligatory requirement; neither will it gain you extra points.
