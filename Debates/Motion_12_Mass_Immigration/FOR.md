# FOR the Motion: "Mass Immigration is a Major Competitive Advantage for a Country"

## Speech (~7-8 minutes)

---

Ladies and gentlemen,

In the 21st century, the most valuable resource on the planet is not oil, not rare earth minerals, not even data. **It is people.** Nations that attract the world's talent, energy, and ambition thrive. Nations that close their doors stagnate. I stand firmly in support of this motion: **mass immigration is a major competitive advantage for a country** — and not merely in theory, but as a documented, measurable, and historically proven reality.

Let me state my position clearly: **the countries that win the future will be the countries that welcome the world. Immigration is not a burden to be managed — it is a strategic asset to be cultivated.**

---

### 1. The Historical Evidence Is Overwhelming — Immigration Built the World's Most Powerful Economy

My first argument draws on the testimony of David Robinson, a former U.S. diplomat who, in his lecture on mass migration, emphasizes that **immigration is central to American identity.** The United States was built through continuous waves of migration, and immigration continues to sustain the country socially, culturally, and economically.

Robinson references public opinion polls showing that approximately **70% of Americans still view immigration positively overall.** This is not sentimentality — it reflects the lived experience of a nation whose greatest economic, scientific, and cultural achievements have been driven by immigrants and their descendants.

Consider the evidence: over **40% of Fortune 500 companies were founded by immigrants or children of immigrants.** Google, Tesla, eBay, Yahoo, WhatsApp — all founded or co-founded by immigrants. The Manhattan Project that ended World War II relied heavily on refugee scientists from Europe. Silicon Valley, the engine of the modern global economy, is inconceivable without immigration.

Robinson's own examples reinforce this point. Arlington County schools in Virginia contain students speaking more than **80 languages.** New York City public schools contain **over 150 spoken languages.** Despite this enormous diversity, American society continues to function and evolve successfully. As Robinson argues, immigration itself is not inherently destabilizing — it is inherently energizing.

---

### 2. Demographic Survival — Immigration Is Not Optional, It Is Essential

My second argument addresses the demographic crisis facing virtually every developed nation. The Financial Times panel discussion on migration features **Martin Wolf**, who acknowledges that migration strengthens the workforce, even if he cautions that migrants eventually age as well.

But Wolf's caution actually reinforces my argument. If immigration only *temporarily* slows demographic aging, then the answer is not less immigration — it is **sustained immigration as a permanent feature of national strategy.** Japan offers the cautionary tale: decades of immigration restriction have produced a shrinking population, a contracting labor force, and economic stagnation. Germany, by contrast, welcomed over one million Syrian refugees in 2015 and has since integrated many into its workforce, helping sustain Europe's largest economy.

The mathematics are simple. Developed nations have fertility rates far below replacement level. Without immigration, their populations will age, their pension systems will collapse, their healthcare systems will be overwhelmed, and their economies will shrink. Immigration is not a choice — **it is a demographic survival strategy.**

---

### 3. Innovation, Entrepreneurship, and the Immigrant Advantage

My third argument focuses on what economists call the "immigrant advantage" — the empirical finding that immigrants are disproportionately entrepreneurial, innovative, and economically productive.

This is not accidental. As **David Robinson** argues in his lecture, migration is fundamentally connected to **"ambition, courage, risk-taking, and hope."** The very act of leaving one's homeland requires precisely the personality traits that drive economic dynamism: tolerance for risk, willingness to work harder, and the ability to see opportunities that others miss. Robinson notes that these are "precisely the characteristics that historically helped build the United States."

The data confirms this insight. Immigrants in the United States are **80% more likely to found a company** than native-born citizens. They file patents at significantly higher rates. They are overrepresented among Nobel Prize winners, among founders of billion-dollar startups, and among recipients of advanced STEM degrees.

Robinson rejects overly selective migration systems that only prioritize highly educated or wealthy migrants, arguing that **"human potential cannot always be predicted"** and that **"courage and resilience matter."** The Somali refugees and the Lost Boys of Sudan he references arrived with nothing — yet contributed enormously. This is the competitive advantage of immigration: it imports not just labor or skills, but **human drive itself.**

---

### 4. Cultural Dynamism and Soft Power

My fourth argument moves beyond economics to the cultural competitive advantage that immigration provides.

The Financial Times panelists, including **Catherine Barnard**, acknowledge that migration transforms local communities. But what the critics frame as disruption, I frame as renewal. The world's most vibrant, creative, and globally influential cities — New York, London, Toronto, Singapore, Berlin — are also the world's most immigrant-dense cities. This is not a coincidence.

Immigration creates cultural cross-pollination that drives creativity in art, cuisine, music, literature, film, and technology. It gives countries **soft power** — the ability to project influence and attract global talent. When the world's best and brightest choose to move to your country, it signals that your society is dynamic, open, and forward-looking. This creates a virtuous cycle: talent attracts talent.

Countries that restrict immigration send the opposite signal. They tell the world that they are closed, fearful, and declining. In the global competition for human capital, **perception matters as much as policy.**

---

### 5. The Counterarguments Are Real but Manageable

My fifth argument directly engages the strongest objections raised in the preparation materials. **David Goodhart**, speaking in the Financial Times panel, warns about rapid demographic change, social cohesion, and public trust. These are sincere concerns, and I do not dismiss them.

But Goodhart's argument is not that immigration is harmful — it is that **poorly managed immigration is harmful.** This is an argument for better policy, not less immigration. Barnard's example of Great Yarmouth, where underfunded public services and poor housing created resentment, illustrates not the failure of immigration but the failure of government investment. When infrastructure keeps pace with population growth, integration succeeds.

Robinson makes the critical distinction between immigration and border control. He argues that **"Americans generally support immigration but become alarmed by perceptions of uncontrolled borders."** The solution is not to reduce immigration but to manage it effectively — through legal pathways, international cooperation, and adequate investment in receiving communities.

The competitive advantage of immigration is not automatic. It requires active policy — language training, credential recognition, social integration programs, and infrastructure investment. But the countries that make these investments reap enormous returns. **The question is not whether mass immigration is an advantage — the question is whether governments are competent enough to realize that advantage.**

---

### Conclusion

Let me conclude by addressing the opposition's deepest fear: that mass immigration threatens national identity. Robinson's lecture provides the perfect rebuttal. He reminds us that **anxiety about immigration has existed throughout American history regardless of the immigrant group involved** — Germans, Irish, Italians, Mexicans, refugees from conflict zones. Benjamin Franklin himself warned that German immigrants would "Germanize" America. Every generation has feared that the current wave of immigration will destroy the nation's character. Every generation has been wrong.

National identity is not a fixed artifact to be preserved behind glass. **It is a living project, continuously enriched by new voices, new perspectives, and new energy.** The countries that understand this — the United States, Canada, Australia, Singapore — have become global powerhouses precisely because they embraced immigration as a competitive advantage.

Martin Wolf is right that migration is inherently political. But the politics should be about how to maximize the benefits, not whether benefits exist. The evidence is clear: countries that attract immigrants grow faster, innovate more, and wield greater global influence than those that do not.

**Mass immigration is not a threat to national competitiveness. It is the single greatest competitive advantage a country can possess.**

I urge you to support this motion. Thank you.
