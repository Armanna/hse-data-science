# AGAINST the Motion: "AI is going to change lives in society for the better"

## Speech (~8 minutes)

---

Ladies and gentlemen,

In January 2020, a man named **Robert Williams** was coming home from work in Detroit when police officers arrested him in front of his wife and two young daughters. He was held for **30 hours** in a detention cell. His crime? An AI-powered facial recognition system said his face matched a shoplifting suspect. It was wrong. Robert Williams is a Black man, and the AI system — trained on datasets that underrepresent dark-skinned faces — had an error rate for darker-skinned women of **34.7%**, compared to just **0.8%** for light-skinned men. He is not alone: at least **eight documented wrongful arrests** from facial recognition errors have occurred in the United States, six of them involving Black individuals.

This is what AI "changing lives" looks like in practice — not the gleaming future of the tech brochures, but a Black father handcuffed in front of his children because an algorithm could not tell his face apart from someone else's.

I stand against this motion: **AI is not going to change lives in society for the better.** It is going to deepen inequality, concentrate power, destroy livelihoods, and threaten the very foundations of human autonomy — all while its champions promise us a utopia that never arrives.

---

### 1. AI Is an Engine of Inequality, Not Equality

My first argument goes to the heart of who benefits from AI and who is harmed by it. The panelist from the model debate who argued for the motion painted a picture of AI as the great democratizer. But the evidence points in the opposite direction: **AI concentrates wealth, power, and advantage among those who already have the most.**

The AI industry is dominated by a handful of corporations — OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta, Anthropic — backed by billions in venture capital, located overwhelmingly in the United States and China. The benefits of AI — the productivity gains, the efficiency improvements, the cost savings — flow primarily to shareholders and executives of these firms. Meanwhile, as AI automates tasks once performed by middle-class workers, the gains accrue at the top and the losses are absorbed at the bottom.

The **NIST study** on facial recognition found that these systems are **10 to 100 times more likely** to misidentify Black and Asian faces compared to white faces. This is not a minor technical glitch — it is the mathematical codification of racial inequality. When biased AI systems are deployed in hiring, lending, criminal justice, and healthcare, they do not reduce discrimination — they **automate and scale it.** As the **Innocence Project** warns, AI is putting innocent people at risk of incarceration. The technology that was supposed to make society fairer is making it more unjust.

---

### 2. The Job Destruction Is Real — And the "New Jobs" Argument Is a Fantasy

My second argument addresses the most immediate threat AI poses to ordinary people: **the destruction of their livelihoods.** My opponents will cite the World Economic Forum's prediction that AI will create 170 million new jobs while displacing 92 million. But let me give you the numbers they will not emphasize.

In the **first six months of 2025 alone, 77,999 tech jobs** were directly attributed to AI displacement. In total, around **55,000 confirmed job losses** have been directly linked to AI through 2025, and the pace is accelerating. **14% of all workers** have already been displaced by AI, with the rate higher among younger and mid-career workers in tech and creative fields. And the burden falls unevenly: **79% of employed women** in the United States work in jobs at high risk of automation, compared to 58% of men.

The panelist from the model debate who argued for the motion claimed that new jobs will replace the old ones — just as happened with previous technological revolutions. But this comparison is dangerously misleading. The automobile revolution took **decades** to play out, giving workers and institutions time to adapt. AI is displacing jobs at a pace that no previous technology has matched. And the "new jobs" that AI creates — machine learning engineers, data scientists, AI ethicists — require years of specialized education that displaced workers cannot easily obtain. A 50-year-old truck driver cannot become a prompt engineer overnight. The promise of "net job creation" is a statistical abstraction that ignores the **real human suffering** of those left behind.

---

### 3. AI Is an Environmental Catastrophe

My third argument is one that the AI industry would prefer you not hear: **AI is consuming the planet's resources at a terrifying rate.**

The carbon footprint of AI systems in 2025 is estimated at **32.6 to 79.7 million tons of CO2** — comparable to the emissions of **New York City.** Data centers consumed about **415 terawatt-hours** of electricity globally in 2024, with AI applications accounting for 15–20% of that. Under the International Energy Agency's projections, data center electricity consumption will **more than double** between 2024 and 2030.

And it is not just energy. Large data centers consume up to **5 million gallons of water per day** — equivalent to the water use of a town of 10,000 to 50,000 people. Each 100-word AI prompt uses approximately **one bottle of water.** At a time when climate change is the defining challenge of our generation, we are building a technology whose environmental footprint is growing exponentially.

The panelist from the model debate who argued for the motion mentioned AI's potential to help solve climate change. But the irony is brutal: **the technology that is supposed to save the planet is actively helping to destroy it.** We cannot AI our way out of a crisis that AI itself is deepening.

---

### 4. The Alignment Problem — AI We Cannot Control

My fourth argument is the most fundamental: **we do not know how to make AI do what we want.** This is not a fringe concern from science fiction enthusiasts. It is the considered judgment of the people who built these systems.

In 2023, **Geoffrey Hinton** — widely known as the "godfather of deep learning" — left Google specifically to warn the public about the dangers of AI, stating he now believes general-purpose AI could arrive within **20 years or less.** **Yoshua Bengio**, another founding figure of modern AI, estimates a **20% probability** that AI development leads to **catastrophic outcomes.** Over **500 prominent researchers and industry leaders** — including the CEOs of OpenAI, DeepMind, and Anthropic — signed a statement declaring that "mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside other societal-scale risks such as **pandemics and nuclear war.**"

Let me repeat that. The people building AI — the people who understand it best — are comparing its risks to **nuclear weapons and global pandemics.** And they are telling us they do not know how to solve the alignment problem: the problem of ensuring that AI systems reliably do what humans intend.

When my opponents tell you AI will change lives for the better, ask them: **better for whom?** And ask them: what happens when an AI system with capabilities we cannot predict pursues goals we did not intend? The honest answer is: nobody knows.

---

### 5. Surveillance, Manipulation, and the Erosion of Autonomy

My fifth argument addresses what AI is already doing to our freedom. AI-powered surveillance systems are deployed in cities worldwide, tracking movements, analyzing behavior, and scoring citizens. In China, AI enables a social credit system that rewards compliance and punishes dissent. But the threat is not confined to authoritarian regimes.

In democratic societies, AI is used by corporations to **manipulate consumer behavior** through hyper-personalized advertising, by political campaigns to micro-target voters with tailored disinformation, and by employers to monitor every keystroke and bathroom break of their workers. The same technology that my opponents celebrate as "personalization" is, in practice, **the most sophisticated system of behavioral manipulation ever created.**

As AI becomes more capable, the asymmetry between those who control these systems and those who are subject to them will only grow. This is not a world where AI has changed lives for the better. This is a world where AI has changed the balance of power — in favor of corporations, governments, and the already-powerful, and against ordinary citizens.

---

### Conclusion

Let me close with a thought about what "better" actually means. My opponents define "better" in terms of efficiency, productivity, speed, and economic growth. By those metrics, AI is indeed impressive.

But a good society is not measured by GDP alone. It is measured by **justice** — and AI deepens injustice. It is measured by **dignity of work** — and AI is destroying livelihoods faster than it creates them. It is measured by **environmental stewardship** — and AI is consuming resources at a catastrophic pace. It is measured by **human autonomy** — and AI is building the most powerful systems of surveillance and manipulation in history. And it is measured by **safety** — and the people who built AI are warning us, in the starkest terms, that they cannot guarantee it is safe.

Robert Williams did not get a better life from AI. He got handcuffs. The 78,000 workers who lost their jobs in the first half of 2025 did not get a better life from AI. They got unemployment. The communities whose water is being drained to cool data centers are not getting a better life from AI.

AI is changing lives, yes. But not for the better. Not for most people. Not in the ways that matter.

Vote against this motion. Thank you.
