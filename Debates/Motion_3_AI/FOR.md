# FOR the Motion: "AI is going to change lives in society for the better"

## Speech (~8 minutes)

---

Ladies and gentlemen,

In 2023, an AI system identified a novel antibiotic candidate in **weeks** — a process that traditionally takes **four to six years.** That one discovery has the potential to save millions of lives from drug-resistant infections, one of the deadliest threats humanity faces this century. This is not science fiction. This is not a future promise. This is AI, working right now, changing lives for the better. And this is just the beginning.

I stand firmly in support of this motion: **AI is going to change lives in society for the better.** Not perfectly. Not without challenges. But overwhelmingly, undeniably, and historically — for the better.

---

### 1. AI Is Already Saving Lives in Healthcare

Let me begin with the domain where the evidence is most powerful and the stakes are highest: **medicine.** The numbers are extraordinary. By 2025, roughly **80% of hospitals** have embraced AI to augment patient care. The U.S. AI healthcare market jumped from $7.72 billion in 2024 to a projected **$99.77 billion by 2033.** But the real story is not in the dollars — it is in the diagnoses.

AI now achieves **93% accuracy** in classifying heart disease. An AI tool successfully detects **64% of epilepsy brain lesions** previously missed by human radiologists — lesions that, if left undetected, could lead to lifelong suffering or death. Machine learning models detect early signs of **breast cancer** in mammograms and identify early **Alzheimer's patterns** in brain scans, years before symptoms appear.

The panelist from the model debate who argued against the motion dismissed these as marginal improvements. But tell that to the woman whose breast cancer was caught at Stage 1 instead of Stage 3 because an algorithm saw what a doctor's eye missed. Tell that to the family whose epileptic child finally received the correct diagnosis. These are not marginal improvements — these are **lives saved, suffering prevented, families kept whole.**

And in drug discovery, AI is collapsing timelines that once seemed immovable. Pharma researchers fast-tracked an AI-discovered serotonin receptor modulator for Alzheimer's psychosis from target identification to Phase I clinical trial in **just one year** — compared to the typical four to six years. When we talk about AI changing lives for the better, this is what we mean: **more cures, faster, for more people.**

---

### 2. AI Is Democratizing Education

My second argument is about education — the great equalizer. The AI education market reached **$8.30 billion** in 2025, with projections of **$88.2 billion** by 2032. But again, the real story is not the money. It is the transformation of what learning can be.

AI is breaking the one-size-fits-all model of education. For the first time in history, we have the technological capacity to provide every student — regardless of income, geography, or disability — with a **personalized learning experience** tailored to their unique pace, style, and needs. A student struggling with algebra in rural India can receive the same quality of adaptive tutoring that was previously available only to students at elite institutions. A child with dyslexia can have text automatically adapted to their reading level. A working mother studying for a degree at night can get instant, intelligent feedback on her essays.

The panelist from the model debate who argued against the motion raised concerns about technology replacing human connection in education. This is a valid concern — but it misunderstands the role of AI. AI is not replacing teachers; it is **liberating them.** By automating grading, administrative tasks, and routine instruction, AI frees teachers to do what they do best: inspire, mentor, and connect with students on a human level. AI handles the repetitive; humans handle the meaningful.

---

### 3. AI Is Supercharging Productivity and Creating New Jobs

My third argument addresses the economic fear that dominates the AI debate: jobs. Let me be direct. **AI is not going to destroy the labor market. It is going to transform it — just as every major technological revolution in history has done.**

The **World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025** is unequivocal: while **92 million jobs** may be displaced by 2030, **170 million new ones** will be created — a **net gain of 78 million jobs.** The Information Technology and Innovation Foundation confirms that employment gains from AI and the data center buildout **dwarf the displacement effects** from automation. This is not wishful thinking — this is the pattern of every technological transition. The automobile destroyed the horse-and-buggy industry and created the entire modern economy. The internet eliminated millions of jobs and created tens of millions more.

And AI is not just creating new jobs — it is making existing workers more productive. Research cited by Stanford's **Human-Centered AI Institute** confirms that AI boosts productivity and, in most cases, helps **narrow skill gaps across the workforce.** Nursing productivity has increased by **30 to 50%** with AI-enabled tools. AI clinical assistants save doctors up to **40% of time** previously spent on paperwork, returning that time to patients. AI is projected to add up to **$2 trillion** to the global economy.

The panelist from the model debate who argued for the motion pointed out that AI is transforming work, and I want to build on that: the transformation is not just economic — it is **liberating.** AI takes over the drudgery — the data entry, the form-filling, the repetitive analysis — and leaves humans free to do creative, strategic, and meaningful work. That is not a dystopia. That is progress.

---

### 4. AI Is Tackling Humanity's Greatest Challenges

My fourth argument looks beyond healthcare and economics to the **existential challenges** facing our species. AI is not just improving individual lives — it is giving us tools to address problems we could not previously solve.

In climate science, AI models are dramatically improving our ability to predict extreme weather events, optimize energy grids, and accelerate the development of new materials for batteries and solar panels. In agriculture, AI-powered precision farming is reducing water waste, cutting pesticide use, and increasing crop yields — critical for feeding a world of 10 billion people. In disaster response, AI systems can analyze satellite imagery in minutes to identify damaged infrastructure and coordinate rescue efforts — tasks that previously took teams of humans days.

These are not hypothetical benefits. They are being deployed **right now,** across dozens of countries, saving resources, reducing emissions, and protecting communities. When we weigh the costs and benefits of AI, we must place these civilizational-scale contributions on the scale.

---

### 5. Addressing the Critics — Risks Are Real, But Manageable

Now, I want to directly engage with the arguments against this motion, because I take them seriously. My opponents will raise three concerns: bias, surveillance, and job loss. Each is legitimate. None is fatal to the case for AI.

**On bias:** Yes, algorithmic bias exists. The case of **Robert Williams** — a Black man wrongfully arrested in Detroit in 2020 due to faulty facial recognition — is a genuine injustice. But here is what the critics miss: **the problem is not AI itself — it is the data and the humans behind it.** Joy Buolamwini and Timnit Gebru's landmark **"Gender Shades" study** exposed the bias — and the response was to improve the systems, diversify the datasets, and pass regulations. AI bias is a solvable engineering and policy problem, not an inherent feature of the technology. And let us not forget: human decision-makers are biased too. Judges, loan officers, hiring managers — decades of research show systematic racial and gender bias in human decisions. AI, at least, can be **audited, measured, and corrected.** Human prejudice cannot be debugged.

**On surveillance:** The fear is real, but the answer is regulation, not rejection. The EU's **AI Act** — the world's first comprehensive AI law — specifically prohibits real-time facial recognition in public spaces and bans social scoring systems. The solution to surveillance is not to abandon AI; it is to govern it.

**On jobs:** I have already shown that the WEF projects a net gain of 78 million jobs. But let me add this: **every previous technology that "threatened" jobs — the printing press, the steam engine, electricity, the computer — ultimately created more prosperity, more opportunity, and more freedom than it destroyed.** There is no reason to believe AI will be different, and considerable evidence that it will follow the same pattern.

---

### Conclusion

Let me close with a simple question. If you were diagnosed with cancer tomorrow, would you want your doctor to have access to an AI system that can detect tumors earlier, recommend treatments based on millions of cases, and cut drug discovery timelines from years to months? If your child were struggling in school, would you want them to have an AI tutor that adapts to their learning style and fills in gaps that an overworked teacher cannot?

The answer — for every person in this room — is yes. And that answer tells you everything you need to know about this motion.

AI is not a threat to be feared. It is a tool to be wielded — wisely, carefully, and with purpose. And when wielded well, it will change lives in society for the better. It already is.

Vote for this motion. Thank you.
