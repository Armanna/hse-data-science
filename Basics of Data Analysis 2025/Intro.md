## 1. Introduction

Understanding the factors that influence student academic success is a central problem in educational data analysis. Academic performance is not determined by a single variable but rather emerges from the interaction of multiple factors such as study habits, attendance, prior achievements, and external academic support.

The goal of this project is to conduct an exploratory data analysis of a dataset describing student characteristics and performance-related attributes. The analysis aims to identify latent structures, relationships between variables, and typical student profiles using classical statistical and data analysis techniques studied in the course *Basics of Data Analysis*.

The project consists of the following components:

### A1 — Two-dimensional regression and correlation analysis
Relationships between selected quantitative variables are examined using scatter plots, correlation coefficients, and simple linear regression. The strength, direction, and interpretability of the observed dependencies are analyzed.

### A2 — Principal Component Analysis (PCA): hidden factor identification and data visualization
Dimensionality reduction is applied to identify latent factors underlying student performance indicators. The contribution of principal components is analyzed, and low-dimensional visualizations are used to interpret the structure of the data.

### A3 — K-means clustering and cluster interpretation
Students are grouped into clusters based on standardized quantitative features. The resulting clusters are interpreted in terms of academic behavior and performance profiles, and the interpretability of different clustering solutions is discussed.

### A4 — Contingency table analysis
Quantitative variables are discretized into categorical levels, and contingency tables are constructed. Conditional probabilities, relative indices, and statistical independence are analyzed to assess associations between categorical features.

### A5 — Bootstrap methods for comparing averages
Bootstrap resampling is used to estimate confidence intervals for sample means and to compare average values between selected groups. Statistical conclusions are drawn based on the obtained intervals.

### A7 — Writing and shaping the analytical report
All results are presented in the form of a structured analytical report. Each method and outcome is explicitly interpreted, and all code used in the analysis is provided in the appendices to ensure transparency and reproducibility.

The primary focus of the project is on interpretation and understanding of the data, rather than on predictive modeling. All results are discussed in detail, and conclusions are drawn with respect to student success factors and learning behavior.
